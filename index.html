How to actually make it work

The setup that matters

  1. Connect your earbuds first, then open this page. The browser picks the audio output at start.
  2. Put the mic closer to the noise than your ears are. Phone on the desk next to the fan / AC / window, not in your hand next to your head. The mic samples the noise, this page inverts it, and the earbuds fire the anti-noise into your ear where it meets the real noise.
  3. Keep the page open and the screen on. Tap the button once and allow microphone access.

Why timing was off, and what changed

  • The old version used a biquad lowpass, which adds frequency-dependent group delay — it smeared the phase and made the anti-noise arrive at the wrong time for different frequencies. That's gone; it's a linear-phase FIR now, so all frequencies are delayed by the same fixed amount.
  • The whole chain is now an AudioWorklet — no graph node scheduling jitter, no hidden buffering, sample-accurate.
  • The browser is asked for the smallest possible buffer (latencyHint: 0).
  • You now have a delay line (0–40 ms, 0.05 ms steps) to manually compensate the remaining latency, plus a polarity flip for when the error is near 180°.
  • Predictive mode trains an LMS filter on the mic signal and forecasts it forward by your chosen look-ahead — this is the only way to beat latency for steady, periodic noise, because it literally predicts the future waveform.

What it can and can't kill

  • Kills well: fans, AC hum, engine drone, road rumble, transformer hum, airplane cabin — steady low-frequency stuff.
  • Won't kill: speech, clatter, doors, sharp transients. That's physics, not a bug in the code.

Order of tuning

  1. Start with Depth 80%, Tone ~950 Hz, delay 0, polarity −.
  2. Tap Auto-align. It sweeps the delay up and down. Tap LOCK the moment the rumble drops away.
  3. Fine-tune with the − / + buttons and the arrow keys.
  4. If the low rumble is steady (fan, engine), turn Predictive mode on and set Look-ahead to roughly the round-trip latency shown at the bottom of this panel.
  5. If the room gets louder or hollow, you've pushed Depth or delay too far — back it down.

If you hear howling

Lower Depth, or move the mic away from the earbuds. The feedback guard auto-ducks the output if it detects a runaway loop, but don't rely on it.
