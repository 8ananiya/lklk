How to actually make it work

Setup that matters

  1. Connect your earbuds first, then open this page. The browser picks the audio output at start.
  2. Put the mic near the noise — phone on the desk next to the fan / AC / window, not in your hand next to your ear. The mic samples the noise, this page inverts it, and your earbuds fire the anti-noise into your ear where it meets the real noise.
  3. Keep the page open and the screen on. Tap the button once and allow microphone access.

What it can and can't kill

  • Kills well: fans, AC hum, engine drone, road rumble, transformer hum, airplane cabin — steady low-frequency stuff.
  • Won't kill: speech, clatter, doors, sharp transients. That's physics, not a bug in the code.

Tuning it

  • Start with Depth 80%, Tone ~900 Hz.
  • If the room gets louder or hollow, you've pushed Depth too far — back it down to ~60%.
  • For pure rumble (plane, fan), drop Tone to 20–30%.

Why it isn't perfect

Real ANC chips run at ~0.05 ms latency. A browser runs at 10–30 ms. Sound has to arrive at your ear exactly out of phase; above roughly 1 kHz the timing error is bigger than a wavelength, so the "anti-noise" starts adding sound instead. That's what the Tone slider is for — it keeps the anti-noise in the band where cancellation actually works.

If you hear howling

Lower Depth, or move the mic away from the earbuds. The feedback guard will auto-duck the output if it detects a runaway loop, but don't rely on it.
