Why you hear an echo, not silence

Active noise cancellation works by playing sound that is the exact inverse of the noise, at the exact same instant. The two waveforms add to zero. If the anti-noise arrives even 1 ms late, it stops cancelling and starts sounding like a slap-back echo. If it arrives very late (which is what Bluetooth does), you hear a clean but delayed copy — which is exactly what you described.

Real ANC chips inside earbuds run at about 0.05 milliseconds of latency. Bluetooth adds 150–300 ms. A browser adds another 10–50 ms. That is 3000–7000× too slow to cancel a waveform directly.

The only thing that actually works in a browser: predicting periodic noise. If a fan spins at a steady rate, we can learn its pattern and forecast it forward by the exact amount of your latency. That's what Predictive: on does. For non-periodic noise (talking, doors, claps), nothing in software can cancel it — the wave is already gone before we can react.

Steps, in order

  1. Connect your earbuds. In the Devices card above, tap Enable & list devices and allow mic access.
  2. Pick your earbuds mic in the first dropdown. If your earbuds appear as two entries, pick the one that says "Headset" — that's the one that has the mic.
  3. Pick your earbuds in the output dropdown (Chrome/Edge only — Safari ignores this).
  4. Tap the big power button. It will start the ANC engine using your selected devices.
  5. Now tap Monitor raw mic. This plays the mic straight to your ears with no processing at all. Clap once. Whatever delay you hear in the monitor is your true round-trip latency. That's your target number.
  6. Turn Monitor off. Set the Look-ahead slider to that number. Set Depth around 60–80%. Set Tone low (deep rumble) if you're cancelling a fan.
  7. Point the mic at the fan / AC and listen. If it gets quieter, you're done. If it gets louder or hollow, lower Depth or nudge Delay.

What it can and can't kill

  • Kills (with prediction): steady fan, AC hum, engine drone, transformer hum, airplane cabin drone.
  • Won't kill — will echo: speech, music, clatter, doors, footsteps, any sudden sound.

If you hear a 10-second echo again

That's not the ANC — that's a buffering / acoustic loop problem:

  • Make sure the earbuds are in your ears and the phone is not on speaker.
  • Turn off the earbuds' own "transparency" / "awareness" mode if they have one — it monitors the room and feeds it back with its own delay.
  • Make sure Monitor raw mic is off. Leaving it on gives you a permanent 100–300 ms echo of everything.
  • Close other apps that use the mic (voice chat, recording).
